## Description (≤4000)
Kilo is Hawaiʻi at a glance. The official warnings, storms, road closures, shelters, weather, earthquakes and volcano status for your island, on one page you can read in ten seconds.

Kilo means to watch, to observe. It was made in Hilo for people who need one answer right now — is the road open, is there a shelter, do I have to leave — and who often need it on one bar of signal.

WHAT IS ON IT

• Now — what is happening on your island, worst first, in plain words: what happened, what to do, how urgent.
• Weather — right now and the week, by town. Surf by shore, buoys, air quality.
• Roads — every closed segment drawn on the island map, the county's own way around when there is one, crashes and signal outages.
• Storms — one sentence for your island, the forecast track, and what to do before it arrives.
• Tsunami — the warning center's own level, siren status, and a one-tap check of whether an address is in an evacuation zone. The zones stay on your phone.
• Volcano — the daily observatory update, alert level, vog and SO₂.
• Earthquakes — everything felt around the islands, with the same numbers the USGS publishes.
• Reports — what neighbors are seeing, clearly marked as unverified, never mixed in with official warnings.

WHEN THE SIGNAL IS BAD

Kilo opens with no signal at all and shows the last copy it saved, with the time it was checked. On a weak connection it loads a 1.5 KB summary first instead of hanging on a page that never arrives. Turn on warnings and the alert itself travels in the notification, so you can read it even when nothing else will load.

PLAIN WORDS

Every item is rewritten as a headline, what to do, and how urgent — Act now, Get ready, Heads up. The agency's own wording is always one tap away, word for word. Nothing is written by a computer. Every item says who reported it and when they last updated it.

WHERE IT COMES FROM

The public feeds published by the National Weather Service, the Pacific Tsunami Warning Center, county Civil Defense, HI-EMA, the Hawaiʻi Department of Transportation, the USGS and the Hawaiian Volcano Observatory, and the Department of Health. Kilo is not affiliated with any of them.

WHAT KILO IS NOT

Kilo is not an emergency service and is not part of any government. In an emergency, call 911. When Civil Defense says something different from Kilo, do what Civil Defense says.

Free. No ads. No account, ever. Kilo does not track you and has nothing to sell. Location is optional, used only to sort what is near you, and never leaves your phone.

Made in Hilo.
